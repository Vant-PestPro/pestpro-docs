Purpose of Visit

This follow-up visit was conducted in connection with our initial inspection and service report dated June 13, 2026 (Report No. PP-2026-WH-001) and the subsequent follow-up visit on August 21, 2026. The purpose of this visit was to check all mechanical traps and monitors placed at the property, re-inspect the primary areas of documented rodent activity, assess any changes to the structural conditions, and update the property contact with our findings.

Access was confirmed through the rear patio. No vehicles were present in the driveway and the guests appeared to be out at the time of service.

Trap Check Results

No Captures Recorded. All mechanical traps placed in the outdoor kitchen and BBQ grill area were inspected and found unsprung with no captures. While this is an encouraging sign that rodents may have moved on from the immediate area, the absence of captures should not be interpreted as resolution of the underlying issue. The open structural penetrations that were documented in the original June 13 report remain unaddressed and continue to present active entry points for rodents from outside the structure.

Three Victor snap traps remain deployed in the BBQ grill alcove, positioned flush to the walls in the primary travel corridors identified during the original inspection. All traps were inspected, repositioned as needed, and left in place for continued monitoring.

Site Conditions & Observations

1. BBQ Grill Alcove (Primary Activity Zone)

The area beneath and around the outdoor BBQ cabinetry continues to show evidence of prior and ongoing rodent activity. Scattered debris and fecal matter remain present on the paver floor in the alcove. The three snap traps placed in this zone are positioned at the key harborage and travel points and are functioning properly. No new captures were recorded since the last visit.

2. Pipe Penetration at Wall Base (Remains Open)

The primary entry point identified in the original inspection, where two PVC pipes (drain and supply lines) pass through the base of the exterior stucco wall, remains open and unsealed. This is the most significant structural gap at the property. Loose debris and shredded material continue to be present at and around the penetration, which is consistent with ongoing rodent use of this access point. No exclusion work has been completed at this location. This must be sealed by a contractor using appropriate rodent-proof materials before the rodent program can be considered effective.

3. Wall Breach Adjacent to Door Frame

The rough opening in the stucco wall at the base of the door frame near the BBQ area has not been properly repaired. A temporary fill material appears to have been placed in the void, but the opening remains accessible and is not a permanent or rodent-proof repair. This location should be patched with mortar or hydraulic cement and reinforced with hardware cloth or copper mesh by a licensed contractor.

4. Patio Screen Door Threshold

We noted a positive change at the screen door: the garden hose that was previously running through the bottom threshold gap has been removed or relocated. This eliminates the previous limitation that prevented a full door sweep seal at that location. The gap along the bottom of the screen door frame and along the base of the adjacent fixed screen panel is now fully accessible for proper sealing with a door sweep or threshold seal. We recommend coordinating this repair with your maintenance team or contractor. The gap is visible at floor level and is large enough to allow rodent passage.

5. Screen Enclosure and Pool Deck Perimeter

The pool deck and surrounding screen enclosure were visually inspected. The screen mesh panels that were repaired by the maintenance team appear intact. There is a gap at the base of the screen frame where the aluminum kickplate meets the paver edge in several locations along the perimeter. These gaps, particularly along the ground-level run adjacent to the grass line, remain as potential secondary entry points for rodents entering the enclosure from the exterior. While these are lower-priority compared to the open pipe penetration, they should be addressed as part of the overall exclusion work.

Recommendations

1. Priority: Seal the Open Pipe Penetration (Contractor Required)

The pipe penetration beneath the BBQ cabinetry is the primary structural entry point at this property. Until it is properly sealed, the rodent program is managing symptoms rather than the source. We recommend sealing this opening with hydraulic cement or appropriate masonry patching compound reinforced with copper mesh or hardware cloth, installed by a licensed contractor. We understand your contractor is scheduled to assess the area during the upcoming guest check-out and encourage that visit to result in a permanent repair.

2. Patch the Wall Breach Near the Door Frame (Contractor Required)

The rough opening in the exterior stucco wall beside the door frame should be properly patched at the same time as the pipe penetration repair. A temporary fill alone is not sufficient. This location also has previously identified exposed wiring that should be evaluated by a licensed electrician during the same contractor visit.

3. Install Door Sweep at Screen Door Threshold

Now that the garden hose has been removed from the screen door threshold, a proper door sweep or threshold seal can be installed. This is a straightforward repair that your maintenance team can complete. A brush-style sweep or rubber threshold seal rated for screen enclosure doors is appropriate for this application.

4. Continue Monitoring — Next Follow-Up in Approximately One Week

We will return in approximately one week for the next scheduled trap check, unless a new rodent sighting is reported, in which case we will respond immediately. We will reassess the treatment plan based on the status of the structural repairs at that time.

Safety Reminder: Three Victor mechanical snap traps are currently active beneath the outdoor BBQ cabinetry. Please ensure all guests, staff, and anyone with access to the outdoor kitchen area are made aware that traps are in place. These devices can cause injury if handled incorrectly and must be kept away from children and pets.
