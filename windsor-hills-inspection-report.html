Reason for Service

This inspection was requested same-day following a report of a rodent observed inside the BBQ grill in the outdoor kitchen area of the property. The guest reported opening the grill lid and seeing a rodent inside, which was likely feeding on food residue from a prior use. Her husband witnessed the rodent exit the grill and move in the direction of the pool area. Beyond this sighting, the guests did not report any additional evidence of activity inside the home.

Pest Pro responded the same day, arriving on-site at 5:15 PM. The scope of the visit was to inspect the reported area, document any evidence of rodent activity, identify potential entry and harborage points, and place initial treatment as appropriate. Our standard emergency rodent service fee of $260.00 was waived for this visit. The service was invoiced at our $120.00 service minimum as a courtesy to Anastasia and Root Investment Properties.

Inspection Summary

Active Rodent Activity Confirmed. The inspection identified multiple indicators of ongoing rodent presence, including a high volume of feces beneath the outdoor grill cabinetry, nesting material at a structural floor penetration, and two open wall penetrations providing direct access into the structure. This is not an isolated incident. The conditions observed are consistent with an established rodent presence that has been active for some time.

The following areas were inspected during this visit: the outdoor kitchen and BBQ grill area, the surrounding screen enclosure, the exterior front of the structure, and the patio screen door entry. All findings are documented below with photo evidence.

Findings and Observations

1. Tree Branches in Contact with the Roofline (Exterior, Front of Structure)

A large tree at the front of the property has branches growing directly into contact with the roofline and soffit. This is one of the most common ways rodents and other pests gain access to a structure. Branches that touch or overhang a roofline act as a natural bridge, allowing rodents to move from the tree canopy directly onto and into the building. Trimming these branches back so they no longer make contact with the structure is something we would recommend coordinating with a landscaper or tree service.

2. Screen Door Gap (Patio Screen Enclosure)

There is a visible gap along the bottom of the patio screen door sweep that is large enough to allow pests and small rodents to pass through. Normally this would be an easy fix with a new weather strip or door sweep. However, this particular door has a garden hose running through the gap, which is used to fill the pool, so a full seal is not practical without relocating the hose access point. We are noting this as a known limitation and flagging it as something to address when the hose routing can be changed.

3. Rodent Feces Isolated to the Grill Cabinetry Area

Rodent droppings were found isolated specifically to the area beneath the outdoor grill cabinetry. No droppings were observed elsewhere in the screen enclosure. The volume and distribution concentrated in that one location are consistent with ongoing, regular activity in and around the grill structure, not a rodent simply passing through. Based on the size and shape of the droppings observed, the activity is more consistent with rat rather than mouse. This is where the guest's initial sighting occurred, and the evidence supports that rodents have been actively using this space for some time.

4. Standing Water Beneath the Grill Cabinetry

A container of standing water was found underneath the BBQ cabinetry. Standing water is a reliable attractant for rodents and other pests. Removing this and ensuring no water is allowed to accumulate in this area will help reduce the conditions that make the space attractive to wildlife.

5. Floor and Wall Penetration with Nesting Material (Primary Entry Point)

A gap was found at the base of the exterior wall where the paver floor and utility pipes meet. This opening appears to connect to the structural wall cavity or extend beneath the foundation. Nesting material was found at and around this penetration, which tells us this is not just a travel corridor but an active harborage site. This is the most significant finding from this inspection and will need to be properly sealed by a contractor using appropriate rodent-exclusion materials.

6. Second Wall Penetration with Exposed Wiring (Safety Concern)

A second opening was found in the exterior stucco wall nearby. This penetration has exposed electrical wiring passing through it, and a wire nut that appears to have come loose was found on the ground below the opening. Beyond being a rodent entry point, this is an electrical safety concern that should be evaluated by a licensed electrician. We are flagging it here so it can be addressed alongside the pest-proofing work.

Full Perimeter Inspection Completed. The entire exterior perimeter of the structure was walked and inspected during this visit. With the exception of the two penetrations documented above, no additional visible entry points or structural gaps were identified.

Treatment Performed

Several professional-grade heavy-duty glue board monitors with rodent attractant applied were placed in the outdoor kitchen area during this visit. Glue board monitors were selected specifically because small children have access to this area. Snap traps, while effective, present a safety risk in that environment. The monitors are positioned flush to the walls in the primary rodent travel corridors, directly adjacent to the areas where the heaviest fecal evidence was found.

All monitors will need to be checked on follow-up. If activity is captured, boards will be replaced. If activity has slowed significantly, we can reassess the treatment plan at that time.

Guest Response: The guest was present during the inspection and expressed that she was satisfied with the response and the service provided.

Our Recommendations

1. Seal the Penetration Points

The two open penetrations beneath the grill cabinetry are the most important thing to address. Until those openings are properly sealed with rodent-exclusion materials, new rodents can continue to enter from outside. We recommend coordinating with a licensed contractor to close both gaps. We are also flagging the second penetration for an electrician to evaluate, given the exposed wiring found there. Additionally, we would suggest looking into a new door sweep or weather stripping for the patio screen door when the hose routing situation allows for it.

2. Tamper-Resistant Bait Stations (Perimeter)

We recommend installing two to four tamper-resistant rodent bait stations along the exterior perimeter of the property at $25.00 per station. These are designed to be safe in guest-accessible outdoor spaces and provide an ongoing layer of perimeter defense to reduce the rodent pressure entering from outside.

3. Follow-Up Service

We recommend scheduling a follow-up visit on or before June 19, 2026. This gives us a chance to check the monitors placed today, assess whether the activity has changed, and make any adjustments to the treatment plan. Ideally this visit happens after the penetrations have been sealed, so we can evaluate how effective the exclusion work has been.
